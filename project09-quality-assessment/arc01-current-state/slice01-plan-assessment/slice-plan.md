## Scope and method

1. Inventory originals and wrappers, map former paths, distinguish duplicates,
   and give every in-scope document a read/disposition record. Recover truncated
   reads. Establish supersession by chronology/evidence without erasing failures.
   Attribute full-document lookup reads to the actual helper context; the
   coordinator checks primary witnesses before making judgments. Do not claim
   that another context's reading was loaded wholesale into this one.
2. Map tracked first-party source, tests, crate/build entrypoints and dependency
   boundaries. Identify exclusions and remaining audit coverage explicitly.
   Exclude private assistant state, vendored/build trees and personal runtime data.
3. Extract stable `OBL-...` IDs with original file/section locators, acceptance
   criteria and lineage. Preserve prospective, deferred and ambiguous scope.
   This is the denominator for later review, not a completion percentage.
4. Assess purpose/scope clarity, architecture assumptions, dependencies,
   integration boundaries, usable increments, observable acceptance,
   traceability, consistent status, explicit decisions and resumption readiness.
   Cite concrete strengths, defects and unknowns. Distinguish original defects,
   later status drift, navigation gaps and missing evidence. Do not invent a
   weighted numerical quality score or penalize historical folder conventions.
5. Recommend how to resume each project and identify unresolved questions.
   Preserve original promises rather than fitting them to the current code.
6. Self-check links, pins, corpus coverage, duplicates and ledger completeness;
   disclose remaining work and bubble-up findings before preparing Slice02.
